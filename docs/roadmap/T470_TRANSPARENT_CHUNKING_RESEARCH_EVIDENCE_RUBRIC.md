# T470 Transparent Chunking Research Evidence Rubric

T470 makes the chunking research basis visible before the next owner docket. It
separates well-supported claims from debated claims and records how each
conclusion was reached, what sources support it, what chunking implication
follows, and what limits remain.

This is non-authorizing. T470 does not choose targets, promote reviewed gold,
write chunk output, create child spans, change route/evaluator behavior, create
graph/retrieval/vector truth, choose a source tradition, decide variants, change
canon scope, or grant theology authority.

## Why This Exists

The T464 comparison and T468 owner decisions produced useful queues, but future
agents need to see why a claim is treated as well-supported, project-scoped, or
debated. Otherwise an agent can accidentally treat "six models agree," "a
chapter boundary exists," "red letters start here," "Strong's metadata is
present," or "an older witness lacks this text" as automatic boundary authority.

T470 gives future packets a transparent table:

- claim,
- support level,
- how concluded,
- source references,
- downstream chunking rule or implication,
- limits.

## Well-Supported Claims

- Parent chunks should prefer discourse and literary units over fixed verse
  counts where the text gives coherent unit signals.
- Unit delimitation can affect exegesis, so boundary choices need rationale.
- Chapter/verse/punctuation/headings/WJ formatting are editorial or reference
  layers and remain evidence-only.
- Model agreement is useful triage evidence, not authority.
- Exact-boundary agreement needs near-boundary or WindowDiff-style diagnostics.
- Variant/source-tradition hot zones need transparent specialist packets.
- The "let the Bible interpret the Bible" principle is the owner's project
  faithfulness posture and must be labeled as such.

## Debated Claims

- Mark 16:9-20.
- John 7:53-8:11.
- Romans 16 doxology placement.
- Deut 32:8-9.
- Ancient delimitation and manuscript-layout inferences.
- Exact Gospel pericope boundaries.
- How strongly model agreement should lower risk.

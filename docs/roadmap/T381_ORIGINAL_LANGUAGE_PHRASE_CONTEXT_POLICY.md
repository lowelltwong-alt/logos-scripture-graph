# T381 Original-Language Phrase/Context Policy

T381 records a non-output-changing lesson: the statement "in Greek the phrase is more important than the word" is useful as a warning against isolated word-study fallacies, but it should not become a new absolute rule.

The governed rule is narrower and safer:

- Greek and Hebrew words matter.
- Lemmas, Strong's-style tags, lexical rarity, morphology, and grammar labels are evidence only.
- Meaning must be reviewed inside phrase, clause, syntax, discourse, author/book, genre, and canonical context.
- No isolated word, lemma, gloss, grammar label, or phrase note authorizes doctrine, chunk boundaries, graph edges, retrieval truth, reviewed gold, route behavior, or output changes.

Future original-language packets must record:

- exact passage scope
- exact surface word or phrase
- lemma/Strong's tag if available
- morphology/parsing if available
- phrase or clause scope
- syntax role
- immediate discourse context
- author/book/genre context
- canonical context needed to avoid proof-texting
- semantic range, not one gloss
- assumptions avoided
- orthodox options preserved
- reviewed-gold dependency
- explicit non-authorizations
- validator/test plan
- non-target identity plan

This policy is especially relevant for John 1:1, Colossians 1:15-20, Titus 2:13, 2 Peter 1:1, John 8:58, Hebrews 1, Matthew 28:19, Genesis 1:26-27, Deuteronomy 6:4, Psalm 110:1, Isaiah 43-44, 1 Corinthians 15:29, and 1 Peter 3:18-4:6.

T381 does not move the project past the T373 owner implementation authorization gate.

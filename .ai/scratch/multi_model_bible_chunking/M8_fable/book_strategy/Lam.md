## §2 Device matrix (byte-swept; consume lam_device_inventory.json + acrostic_spine.json, don't re-derive)

### §2a The acrostic spine (tier-1: inclusio_refrain_acrostic) — literary_form_decision_matrix
The formal skeleton of chs 1-4 is the alphabetic acrostic: chs 1, 2, 4 one verse per letter; ch 3
three verses per letter (uniform triplets byte-verified); ch 5 = 22 verses, NOT acrostic. Letter
order is a byte fact: ch 1 ayin-then-pe (standard); chs 2, 3, 4 PE-THEN-AYIN (reversed). The
letter is a text signal; the verse division is tier-4. Consequences: (i) poem boundaries 1:1, 2:1,
3:1, 4:1, 5:1 are tier-1 seams of the highest order (a fresh alef line + eikhah onset at 1:1, 2:1,
4:1; ani ha-gever at 3:1; zekhor YHWH at 5:1); (ii) inside chs 1, 2, 4 a row seam always falls ON a
letter boundary (= a verse boundary) — never mid-verse; (iii) inside ch 3 a row seam falls ON a
TRIPLET boundary (3:1, 3:4, 3:7 … 3:64) unless a disclosed tier-1 reason cuts inside a triplet;
(iv) the letter sequence is INCLUSIO-grade evidence for the poem as a whole and is disclosed as such
in every row of chs 1-4 (which letters the span covers, in the witness's order).

### §2b Voice and address seams (tier-1: scene_participant_location_change + discourse_frames)
Lamentations argues its internal seams by SPEAKER and ADDRESSEE, not by messenger formulas (there
is no koh-amar; the divine voice never speaks). The byte-swept signals: third-person description of
the city vs first-person city speech (ch 1: the narrator through 1:11; Zion's voice from 1:12 "Is it
nothing to you"; the 1:9c / 1:11c interjections as early first-person intrusions); direct address
TO YHWH (imperatives reeh 6 vv / habitah 2 vv / zekhor 3 vv; the ch-5 prayer); the narrator's own
first person in ch 2 (2:11 "my eyes fail"; 2:13 address to the daughter of Jerusalem; 2:18-19
imperatives to the wall/daughter of Zion; 2:20-22 Zion's prayer); ch 3 the geber's lament (3:1-18),
the turn to hope (3:19-39), the communal "let us" (3:40-47), the return to the individual (3:48-66);
ch 4 the narrator (4:1-16), the communal "we" (4:17-20), the Edom/Zion apostrophe (4:21-22); ch 5
the communal petition (5:1-18) and the close (5:19-22). Personification formulas are byte censuses:
bat-Tsiyon 8, bat-ammi 5, bat-Yehudah 3, bat-Yerushalam 2, betulat-bat 2, bat-Edom 2 verses.

## §8 Register + hygiene (verbatim into every brief)

file-order talk, review-actor names, tool filenames, session/wave talk, or staged-file
names/stems. The "(sweep: N verses)" convention is the one sanctioned citation shorthand.

hand-typed, never copied through the draft (E-01 nfd degradation is HARD at the writer gate).
Every universal claim (only/never/first/last/each/sole/densest/unique/nowhere/no-other …) carries an
adjacent DIGIT-BEARING sweep citation naming the swept OBJECT and UNIT; exclusivity claims are
NEVER tier-dampened (E-16). Tier-label every recurrence claim; "verbatim/byte-identical" only per
collate truth with the tier named. Form-class labels (imperative, jussive, participle, gender,
person) are byte-checkable claims — same discipline as digits; the staged extract has NO morphology
layer. Gloss extent = splice extent (E-08). Read back every splice in its sentence (E-04).
Semantic-class count discipline: name the swept object FIRST, then count; blended sweeps forbidden.
Cross-seam cohesion: a byte-true device straddling the row's own seam argues continuity against the
row unless disclosed. The acrostic letters a span covers are disclosed in every ch 1-4 row (letter
names in the witness's order; ch-3 triplets named as triplets). observed_substrate_signals uses the
dotted signal-key taxonomy (acrostic.*, voice.*, address.*, refrain.*, petition.*, closure.* …);

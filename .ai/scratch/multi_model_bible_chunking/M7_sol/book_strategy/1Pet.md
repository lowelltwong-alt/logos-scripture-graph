## Greek, textual, translation, speaker, and form hot zones

- `1:1-2`: diaspora/election/sanctification-obedience-sprinkling syntax; no audience-identity or doctrinal selection.
- `1:3-12`: blessing syntax, new birth/living hope/inheritance, trial-purpose and unseen-love chain, salvation wording, prophetic inquiry, Spirit-of-Christ phrasing. Test `1:10` as transition without detaching it from the opening movement.
- `1:13-25`: inferential `dio`, hope/holiness imperatives, citations, impartial-judge/sojourning logic, ransom imagery, sincere love, imperishable seed and Isaiah citation. Preserve exhortation and grounds.
- `2:1-10`: vice-removal/growth chain, `logikon adolon gala`, living stone and scripture catena, honor/shame contrast, people-title citations. Test 2:1–3 against both adjacent units without sacramental, ecclesial, or supersessionist selection.
- `2:11-17`: beloved address, resident-alien imagery, conduct among nations, visitation wording, submission to human institution/creature, emperor/governors, freedom and honor list. No political policy.
- `2:18-25`: household-servant address, unjust suffering and grace/credit vocabulary, Christ example, passion citations/allusions, shepherd/overseer close. Preserve address plus example; no slavery, atonement, or office policy.
- `3:1-7`: household address, participial continuity, adornment contrast, Sarah example, “weaker vessel” wording, co-heirs/prayer close. No gender or household policy.
- `3:8-17`: communal virtue list, blessing/citation, zeal/suffering conditional, fear/sanctify/apology wording, conscience and good conduct. Test 3:13–17 while preserving its link to 3:8–12.
- `3:18-22`: variants in Christ’s suffering/death wording, flesh/spirit datives, “spirits in prison,” Noah chronology, baptismal antitype and conscience/appeal/pledge translation, ascent/session/powers. Preserve alternatives; no descent, afterlife, baptismal, atonement, or Christological ruling.
- `4:1-6`: `oun` link to 3:18, arm-yourself metaphor, suffering/cessation wording, former-life vice catalogue, judge living/dead, gospel-to-dead wording and flesh/spirit contrast. No afterlife or moral-policy selection.
- `4:7-11`: end frame, sober prayer, love/hospitality, gift/stewardship speech/service pair, purpose clause and doxology. Keep register together unless the doxology proves a seam.
- `4:12-19`: fiery-trial address, participation/glory contrast, Spirit-of-glory wording, suffering-label list, “Christian,” judgment beginning with house, citations, entrust-souls close.
- `5:1-5`: elder appeal, shepherd/oversight variant, willing/eager/not-lording contrasts, chief-shepherd image, younger submission, mutual humility and citation. No office polity.
- `5:6-11`: humility/anxiety, watchful adversary-lion, resistance, worldwide suffering, restoration promise, dominion/doxology. Test smaller routes while preserving the closing movement.
- `5:12-14`: Silvanus delivery attestation, true-grace exhortation, “she in Babylon,” Mark, kiss, peace. No location, identity, authorship, or dating selection.

## Internal-Bible and ancient-context control

Use Torah, Psalms, Prophets, Gospels, Acts, other epistles, Revelation, Second Temple materials, Greco-Roman letter/household conventions, later Jewish/rabbinic material, and patristic reception only for form, citation, lexical range, discourse parallels, and reception evidence. Scripture catenae and passion language may expose cohesion but never authorize harmonization or doctrine. Ancient household and imperial context is evidence only and cannot decide contemporary policy.

## Premortem

Highest risks are retaining chapter frames; splitting every citation or imperative; detaching suffering examples from their addresses; making 2:1–3, 3:13–17, 4:7–11, or 5:6–11 too small; forcing one interpretation of 3:18–22 or 4:6; smuggling political, household, baptismal, atonement, afterlife, office, audience, authorship, or identity claims; and treating doxologies as automatic seams. Preserve exact competing routes and the larger parent whenever uncertainty remains.

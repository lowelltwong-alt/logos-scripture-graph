# Genesis book strategy — M7 Sol independent candidate

## selected_strategy

Use `literary_marker_aware_v2` with the T467 larger-unit overlay. Read the entire book directly in canonical order, identify the book's recurrent *toledot* transitions and scene movements, preserve complete narrative episodes and embedded forms, and use WEB paragraph/poetry/footnote metadata plus the Hebrew observation substrate only as non-authorizing evidence. Prefer a larger coherent unit when two smaller units would lose a setup/payoff, repeated-report, speech-response, genealogy, treaty, blessing, or journey-transition relationship.

## literature_type_or_mixed_genre

Genesis is sustained Hebrew narrative containing creation sequence, genealogies and family registers, etiological notices, covenant and treaty scenes, dreams and interpretations, blessings/poetry, travel reports, death/burial notices, and concentrated direct speech. Embedded poems remain with their governing narrative unless the poem itself constitutes the complete literary event.

## literary_form_decision_matrix

| Form | Default treatment | Genesis applications |
|---|---|---|
| Narrative episode | Keep inciting action, dialogue, reversal, and closure together | Eden; flood; Hagar; Sodom; binding of Isaac; Dinah/Shechem; Judah/Tamar; Joseph tests |
| Genealogy/register | Preserve the complete register and its formulaic closure | Genesis 5; 10; 11:10–26; 25:12–18; 36; 46:8–27 |
| Embedded poem/blessing | Keep with the speech-act and narrative frame; do not split at every poetic line | 3:14–19; 4:23–24; 9:25–27; 25:23; 27:27–40; 48:15–16; 49:1–28 |
| Repeated report/dream | Preserve dream/report/interpretation or command/fulfillment relationships | Genesis 24; 40–41; 42–45 |
| Treaty/covenant scene | Keep negotiation, sign/witness, and departure/closure together | 21:22–34; 26:26–33; 31:22–55 |
| Journey/transition | Attach short bridge material to the movement it launches or closes | 11:27–32; 27:46–28:9; 46:28–47:12 |
| Death/burial notice | Preserve preparation, death, mourning, transport, and burial when they form one movement | 23; 49:29–50:14 |

## substrate_markers_considered

The WEB witness supplies dense `p` evidence throughout Genesis; `q1/q2` marks 3:14–19, 4:23–24, 9:25–27, 27:27–40, 48:15–16, and 49:2–27; `b` subdivides the tribal blessing in Genesis 49; and `nb` occurs at 50:1. These markers were checked against direct literary reading. They support local form recognition but do not independently authorize boundaries. The risk index marks 46 footnoted verses and 46 poetry/liturgy verses; special attention is therefore given to translation-sensitive or mixed-form regions rather than mechanically cutting at every marker.

## strongs_metadata_considered_evidence_only

Hebrew Strong's identifiers and observation flags were inspected only to identify places requiring a language-aware review. They are not definitions, roots are not meanings by themselves, and neither Strong's numbering nor a lemma transition creates a boundary. The review order is phrase, clause, syntax, discourse, book context, then canonical context.

## source_metadata_evidence_only_check

WEB paragraphs, poetry layout, footnotes, headings, notes, cross-references, Strong's tags, lemmas, and morphology are witnesses to investigate. None is treated as an ancient canonical division, theological authority, or substitute for Hebrew discourse judgment. Genesis has no WEB editorial cross-reference rows in the canonical cross-reference file; later canonical reuse must therefore be assessed by reviewers without reverse-engineering boundaries from an English editor.

## larger_unit_preservation_check

The plan deliberately keeps the creation week (1:1–2:3), Eden (2:4–3:24), the flood rise/recession/exit movement (7:17–8:19), the Table of Nations (10), major genealogies/registers, the Isaac-in-Gerar sequence through treaty closure (26:1–33), Dinah/Shechem (34), Esau/Edom registers (36), Joseph's prison dream scene (40), and Jacob's tribal blessing (49:1–28) intact. Long spans are accepted where subdivision would detach a response from its initiating action or fragment a list's literary function.

## list_register_function_check

Genealogies and registers are treated as literary architecture, not expendable data. Genesis 5, 10, 11:10–26, 25:12–18, 36, and 46:8–27 remain whole. Genesis 29:31–30:24 remains one child-naming register embedded in sibling rivalry. Genesis 35:16–29 remains a family-transition ensemble because Rachel's death, the twelve-son summary, Jacob's return, and Isaac's death close the preceding cycle and hand off to the Esau and Jacob histories.

## epistle_unit_check_if_applicable

Not applicable: Genesis is not an epistle. The analogous guard is to preserve complete speeches, blessings, repeated reports, and their narrative responses.

## over_split_risk_check

Highest over-split risks are creation-day paragraphs, Eden's speech sequence, flood chronology, the Genesis 10 and 36 registers, Genesis 24's repeated commission/report, the framing notices around the stolen blessing (26:34–28:9), the Genesis 29–30 child register, Laban's pursuit/treaty, Genesis 34, repeated dreams in 40–41, the staged recognition narrative in 42–45, the migration register in 46, and the blessing poem in 49. Short chunks are retained only when they are genuine transition notices or complete scenes.

## sidecar_specificity_plan

Any `medium_low` or `low` chunk will receive matching rows in all three required sidecars naming the exact competing boundaries, Hebrew/translation problem, literary dependency, observed markers, downstream risk, and reviewer type. Material reviewer disagreement is recorded in the review packet. A Sol ruling does not erase dissent: a specialist may append a reasoned appeal with evidence and requested remedy. An unresolved appeal remains low confidence and is routed as `deferred_human_or_external_ai`.

## chapter_only_fallback_reason_if_used

No chapter-only fallback is used merely because a chapter boundary exists. A chunk may coincide with a whole chapter only after a book-level literary judgment that the chapter is itself a coherent episode or register.

## expected_low_confidence_regions

- 2:4–3:24: mixed narrative and embedded poetry; translation-sensitive heading/syntax at 2:4.
- 6:1–8: notoriously compressed Hebrew referents and lexical choices in 6:1–4; 6:5–8 supplies the narrative appraisal and flood transition.
- 26:34–28:9: overlapping wife/grief, blessing, flight, and departure frames across modern chapter divisions.
- 31:1–55: dream report, household-god episode, legal protest, and treaty closure invite competing internal seams.
- 35:16–29: several brief family notices function cumulatively as a transition.
- 44:1–45:15: cup test, Judah's speech, and recognition form a tightly dependent sequence with plausible alternate seams.
- 47:27–50:26: deathbed scenes, embedded poetry, burial, reconciliation, and final death notices require cross-chapter boundaries.

## frontier_or_atlas_candidate_expectations

Genesis is not a universal frontier book, but Genesis 6:1–8 is a mandatory local atlas candidate because English renderings can obscure clause relationships and referents. The 26:34–28:9 frame, 35:16–29 transition, and 44:1–45:15 recognition sequence are review candidates if independent specialists disagree. Promotion authority remains none.

## Independent review and appeal route

Every frozen chunk decision receives two blind reviews: one literary/form review and one Hebrew/translation/canonical-context review. A distinct peer checker challenges both reviews; Sol answers each challenge and may request a high-level adjudication on material disputes. The original reviewers then see the ruling and may accept it or append an appeal. Appeals preserve the proposed boundary, evidence, claimed harm, response to the ruling, confidence, and requested human/AI disposition. Consensus is not required for completion; unresolved dissent is explicit, low-confidence, and non-authorizing.

# 1 Chronicles literary strategy — candidate evidence only

Candidate-only, non-authorizing, independent of M1–M6/comparison/T417. Read 1Chr.1.1–29.30 as a sequence of ancestry, clan, settlement, military, cultic-personnel, and postexilic-resettlement registers (1–9), followed by Saul’s terminal battle and David-centered narrative/administrative complexes (10–29). Chapter numbers and English headings are never boundary authority. Prefer the larger coherent register, scene, speech-response, battle-result, transport-crisis-resolution, census-plague-site-resolution, assembly-address-prayer-response, or succession/death unit when a proposed split would orphan its governing function or outcome.

Use explicit ancestry heads, repeated “sons/descendants/genealogy” formulas, tribe or clan shifts, settlement and office functions, narrative actor/location/time resets, direct-speech frames, battle or accession closures, list-purpose changes, ark-transport stages, liturgical speech and response, prophetic message and royal response, construction preparation, public assembly, charge, prayer, offering/acclamation, succession, and death/source notices. ## Larger-unit preservation check — larger_unit_preservation_check

Preserve the ancestry funnel from humanity to Israel while allowing genuine heading/function changes; Judah’s branching clan register; Simeon’s settlement-expansion account; transjordanian tribal registers and exile notice; Levi’s descent, priestly line, cultic appointments, settlements, and Aaronic cities by their stated functions; remaining tribal genealogies; Benjamin-to-Saul ancestry and the Jerusalem resettlement/office register; Saul’s battle, death, burial, and narrated interpretation; David’s accession and Jerusalem capture; warrior lists with their framing exploits; ark movement from failed transport through successful procession and installed worship; Nathan’s message and David’s prayer; wars and administrative summaries; the census, plague, threshing-floor resolution, and temple-site identification; David’s preparations, Solomon charge, Levite/priest/singer/gatekeeper/treasury/military-administrative registers; and the final assembly, public charge, prayer, gifts, Solomon’s accession, and David’s death/source closure.

## List/register function check — list_register_function_check

The first nine chapters are not disposable name blocks. For each register identify its governing ancestor, tribe, clan, geography, office, cultic service, military muster, resettlement, or royal-line function. Split only when the governing function changes; do not atomize each name chain. In chapters 23–27, distinguish Levite organization, Aaronide divisions, musicians, gatekeepers, treasuries/officials, military courses, tribal leaders, royal property managers, and royal counselors while preserving each complete roster with its introduction and closure.

Require WEB↔MT mapping. Flag without deciding: name spellings and compressed descent formulas throughout 1–9; Edomite royal/chief lists in 1; Judah/Caleb/Jerahmeel relationships and parentage formulas in 2–4; Jabez wording in 4; land, war, exile, and Assyrian names in 5; high-priestly sequence, tabernacle/temple roles, and Levitical cities in 6; Benjamin/Saul duplications and Jerusalem resettlement in 7–9; Saul’s death and body/burial vocabulary in 10; parallel-account pressure throughout David narratives; mighty-warrior names/numbers/exploits in 11–12; Perez/Uzza wording and ark terminology in 13–16; covenant/house/seed/rest language and David’s prayer in 17; war geography, tribute, census and casualty numbers in 18–21; “Satan/adversary,” angel/plague, threshing floor, fire, altar, and site identification in 21–22; ages, duties, divisions, lots, offices, names, numbers, and overlapping functions in 23–27; assembly speeches, written “pattern,” offerings, enthronement language, regnal totals, and source notices in 28–29. No harmonization with Samuel–Kings, preferred reading, chronology, genealogy reconstruction, authorship, cultic/royal authority, providential explanation, or theological ruling.

## Over-split risk check — over_split_risk_check

Highest risks: treating chapters 1–9 as chapter-sized or name-by-name blocks; detaching Saul’s death from burial/interpretive closure; separating warrior rosters from accession/capture or exploit frames; splitting the ark crisis from its immediate outcome or successful transport from installed worship; separating Nathan’s oracle from David’s prayer; orphaning war outcomes or administrative summaries; splitting census, plague, intercession, site resolution, and temple-preparation transition; atomizing chapters 23–27 by every subheading; and separating the final assembly’s charge, prayer, offering, acclamation, succession, and Davidic closure without form evidence.

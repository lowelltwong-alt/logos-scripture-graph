# 2 Timothy independent literary-chunking strategy — M7_sol

## Scope and authority

Replace the four chapter-frame fallbacks with a candidate-only literary map independent of M1–M6, `comparison/`, and T417. Segment epistolary, thanksgiving, remembrance, exhortation, example catalogue, paraenesis, faithful-saying/confessional, vice-list, charge, autobiographical testimony, travel/request, greeting, and benediction forms. Do not decide authorship, date, chronology, office/succession policy, canon, textual witness, doctrine, theology, or historical reconstruction. All contested routes remain LOW and `deferred_human_or_external_ai`.

## Larger literary architecture

Test and preserve these complete parents:

1. `2Tim.1.1-2Tim.1.18` — prescript; thanksgiving/remembrance; gift/courage exhortation; suffering/testimony/deposit charge; regional and household examples.
2. `2Tim.2.1-2Tim.2.26` — entrusting and soldier/athlete/farmer images; remembrance/gospel/suffering and faithful saying; approved-worker/quarrel warning and foundation; vessels, flight, pursuit, and servant correction.
3. `2Tim.3.1-2Tim.3.17` — last-days vice list and opponent examples; contrasted following/suffering; sacred writings/scripture and equipment conclusion.
4. `2Tim.4.1-2Tim.4.22` — solemn proclamation charge; departure/crown testimony; personal requests, abandonments, defense/rescue, doxology; greetings and grace close.

Prefer larger coherent units where a saying, command, metaphor chain, list, example, personal request, or closing movement would otherwise be detached. Chapter numbers are never sufficient evidence.

## Greek, textual, translation, and discourse hot zones

- `1:3-7`: thanksgiving syntax, ancestral/conscience language, remembrance clauses, family-faith examples, gift/laying-on wording, and spirit/power/love/discipline chain.
- `1:8-14`: shame/suffering imperatives and grounds, calling/grace/appearance clauses, speaker self-description, `parathēkēn mou` referent/translation, pattern/deposit language. No doctrine or succession policy.
- `1:15-18`: scope/function of abandonment and household example; no historical reconstruction beyond the transmitted discourse.
- `2:1-7`: grace strengthening, entrusting-to-faithful-people chain, soldier/athlete/farmer metaphors, imperative/understanding close; no office lineage policy.
- `2:8-13`: remembrance formula, good-news/seed/raised wording, chains/endurance rationale, faithful-saying or poetic lineation, pronoun and deny/remain-faithful clauses.
- `2:14-19`: quarrel/logomachy warning, `orthotomounta`, overthrow/foundation contrast, inscription/citation form, names; do not decide a doctrinal slogan.
- `2:20-26`: household vessels metaphor, flee/pursue imperative chain, youthful desires/addressee scope, servant traits, repentance/knowledge/snare syntax.
- `3:1-9`: last-days frame, vice-list grammar and closure, household infiltration description, learning/knowledge clauses, named comparison and later reception; no identity/history inference.
- `3:10-17`: following contrast, persecution narrative chain, evil progression, continue/learned/from-whom clauses, sacred writings, and `pasa graphē theopneustos` syntax/translation; no canon or inspiration-doctrine proof-text decision.
- `4:1-5`: solemn charge syntax and textual pressure, appearing/kingdom relation, proclamation imperatives, future desire/teacher accumulation, sobriety/suffering/ministry fulfillment.
- `4:6-8`: `spendomai`, departure, fight/race/faith perfects, crown/judge/appearing close; no chronology or biography authority.
- `4:9-18`: request chain, named movements, Galatia/Gaul and other textual pressures, usefulness/request objects, first-defense abandonment, rescue/doxology sequence; no external biography reconstruction.
- `4:19-22`: greetings, illness/travel notices, season request, singular/plural benediction shifts, textual forms; no chronology selection.

## Internal-Bible and ancient-context control

Use Torah, Prophets, Psalms/Wisdom, Gospel traditions, Acts, other epistles, Revelation, Second Temple texts, and later Jewish/patristic reception only for direct citation, form, metaphor, list, lexical range, or discourse analogy. Later names/traditions around 3:8 are reception evidence only. No relation forces boundary symmetry, identity, history, office structure, canon, doctrine, or theology.

## Premortem

Highest risks: four chapter fallbacks; splitting metaphor/saying/list units into proof texts; turning 2:2 into succession policy; using 2:11-13 or 3:16 to settle doctrine/canon; using later tradition to identify 3:8 figures historically; treating every name in 4:9-18 as a new chunk; reconstructing chronology; and separating request, defense, rescue, doxology, greetings, or grace without form evidence. Preserve exact finer and complete larger routes; force no consensus.

# Ecclesiastes literary strategy — candidate evidence only

Candidate-only, non-authorizing, independent of M1–M6/comparison/T417. Read Ecclesiastes 1:1–12:14 as framed wisdom discourse containing a superscription, opening poem, first-person investigations, better sayings, observations, enjoyment refrains, poems and an epilogue. Chapter numbers and modern headings are never boundary authority. Prefer a complete investigation, poem, saying chain, observation/refrain movement or frame speech; preserve the larger discourse when voice or seam is disputed.

## Literary-form decision matrix — literary_form_decision_matrix

Use explicit frame formulas, first-person quest resumptions, “I saw/said/turned/returned” discourse shifts, rhetorical questions, `hebel` and “under the sun” recurrences, toil/enjoyment conclusions, “better than” chains, numbered observations, imperatives, royal/court counsel, time/aging poems, speaker evaluation and epilogue formulas. Repeated vocabulary, paragraphing or chapters alone are not seams.

Preserve cross-chapter units and larger exact parents whenever internal boundaries are disputed.

No preferred translation, speaker/editor reconstruction, authorship, chronology, historicity, moral system, afterlife doctrine, gender verdict, contradiction harmonization, Christology or theology.

## T467 and premortem checks

Every chapter-coincident span needs positive local form/closure independent of numbering. Highest risks are chapter fallback; detaching enjoyment refrains from the investigation they conclude; splitting 5:1–7 at the English chapter seam; atomizing “better” sayings; assigning every voice shift to a hypothetical editor; treating `hebel` as one fixed English concept; extracting famous verses; forcing a single systematic doctrine; and separating 12:9–14 from its epilogue function. When experts disagree, retain the larger unit, lower confidence and append the appeal.

## Over-split risk check — over_split_risk_check

Protect the complete first-person experiment, linked better-saying chain, observation plus refrain, cross-chapter youth/aging poem and epilogue. Opposite under-split risk is retaining all twelve chapters without the title, opening poem, time poem, cross-chapter movements and final frame seams.

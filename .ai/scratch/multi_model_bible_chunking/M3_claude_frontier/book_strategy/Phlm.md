# Philemon — M3 frontier chunking strategy (pilot-fragile book)

**Strategy id:** `literary_marker_aware_v2` (frontier pass). **Literature type:** a single-chapter
personal epistle (`genre_epistles`, 25 verses): Paul's appeal to Philemon to receive the runaway
slave Onesimus as a brother. Philemon is a pilot-fragile book, so the units are flagged.

## Markers / evidence
Prose (`p`/`f` only). `has_strong_g` — Strong's **Greek evidence only**. `has_footnote` — evidence only.

## Boundary handling (independent rationale)
Rather than one whole-book block, the letter is chunked into its **epistolary rhetorical units**:
greeting (1:1–3); thanksgiving for Philemon (1:4–7); the appeal for Onesimus (1:8–20); the closing
and final greetings (1:21–25). This sub-chapter division follows the standard letter form and avoids
a silent whole-chapter block in a pilot-fragile book.

## Strong's / WJ handling (evidence only)
Strong's Greek is **evidence only**; never used to set a boundary or decide meaning. `wj` n/a.

## Low-confidence & frontier escalation triggers
The units are flagged `medium_low` (pilot-fragile book). The appeal (1:8–20) carries the
slavery/brotherhood-ethics review load; surfaced, not encoded in the boundary.

## Why this is not silent chapter-only
The single chapter is split into four rhetorical units by the letter's own form — the deliberate
opposite of a whole-chapter block — with each unit's evidence naming its epistolary function.

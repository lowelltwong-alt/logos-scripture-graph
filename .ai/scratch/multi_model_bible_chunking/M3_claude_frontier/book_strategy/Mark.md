# Mark — M3 frontier chunking strategy

**Strategy id:** `literary_marker_aware_v2` (frontier pass). **Literature type:** Gospel
(`genre_gospels`): fast-paced narrative pericopes ("immediately"), controversy cycles, parables, an
Olivet discourse (13), and a passion narrative, with the **disputed longer ending** (16:9–20).

## Local marker signals (Rust substrate)
- `has_wj` dense in teaching/discourse (ch12 wj=76; ch13 wj=78) — **evidence only**. `has_crossref`/`x`
  (OT citations); `has_variant_reading` on **ch16** (the longer-ending textual crux). `has_strong_g`
  — Strong's **Greek evidence only**.

## Boundary handling (independent rationale)
- Chunked by **pericope / controversy cycle / discourse**, with arcs (2:1–3:6 five controversies;
  4:35–5:43 miracle triad; 8:27–9:1 confession-and-cost). The Olivet discourse (13) is a unit.
- **ch16 is split at 16:8 / 16:9** to isolate the empty-tomb ending (16:1–8) from the longer ending
  (16:9–20), which the manuscripts mark as textually uncertain.

## Strong's / WJ handling (evidence only)
WJ/red-letter and Strong's Greek are **evidence only** — never used to set a boundary or decide
christology; discourse seams follow the narrative frame.

## Low-confidence & frontier escalation triggers
- **16:9–20 flagged** as a major **textual variant** (the longer ending), and 16:1–8 flagged for the
  abrupt-ending question. The Olivet discourse (13) flagged for eschatology. Surfaced, not decided.

## Why this is not silent chapter-only
Pericope/cycle seams (with several arcs) drive boundaries, and the ch16 split isolates the textual
crux rather than blurring it into the chapter.

# Book Strategy — Isaiah (M2_claude_sonnet5)

- **strategy_id:** literary_marker_aware_v2 | **book:** Isa | **pilot_book:** false

## Selected strategy
Section/oracle-based chunking following Isaiah's well-established macro-structure: judgment
oracles against Judah/Jerusalem (1-12, including the Book of Immanuel), oracles against the
nations (13-23), the Isaiah Apocalypse (24-27), further woe oracles (28-35), the historical
narrative interlude (36-39, paralleling 2Kgs.18-20), the Book of Comfort with the four Servant
Songs (40-55), and the closing chapters (56-66). Named-nation oracle chapters (13-23) and closely
related oracle pairs are grouped where the text's own headings mark one continuous oracle.

## Literature type / mixed genre
prophetic_oracle throughout, with narrative sections at the call vision (6), Immanuel narrative
(7), and the historical interlude (36-39).

## Substrate markers considered
Prose (non-poetry-flagged) chapters identified directly from the substrate (7, 8, 12-25, 27-29,
36-37, 39) and used to inform the narrative/oracle literature-type distinction rather than assumed.

## Strong's metadata — evidence only
Not used to resolve any interpretive question.

## Chapter-only fallback
Not used as a default; most boundaries follow named-oracle or thematic-section structure and
frequently span or subdivide chapter lines (e.g., 11:1-12:6, 24:1-27:13, 36:1-37:38).

## Expected low-confidence / doctrinally sensitive regions
The Immanuel sign (7:14), the child/son oracle (9:6-7), the shoot from Jesse (11:1-10), the
Lucifer/Day Star taunt (14:12-15), the four Servant Songs (42:1-4, 49:1-6, 50:4-9, 52:13-53:12),
Cyrus named as the LORD's anointed (44:28-45:1), the anointed messenger of 61:1-3, and the new
heavens/new earth promise (65:17) — all carry long, cross-tradition-disputed interpretive
histories, flagged for downstream awareness, not adjudicated here.

## Frontier / atlas candidate expectations
Roughly 13 rows expected — Isaiah concentrates more historically significant, cross-tradition-
disputed theological content than any Wave 4 book so far, given the Servant Songs and messianic-
reading texts.

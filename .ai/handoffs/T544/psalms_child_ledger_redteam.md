## Form-label red-team findings

1. Labels that merely restate a child’s chosen function are unsupported until the rationale names the transition. Highest-risk examples are the highly composite labels in Ps 18, 37, 68, 104, 107, 136, 144, and 145. A label is a retrieval convenience, not a form finding.
2. `refrain_cycle` is defensible in Psalms 46, 57, 80, 107, and 136 only when the actual repeated WEB wording and its position are named. It is not defensible merely because the label says “refrain.”
3. Psalm 119 octets are the exception with strong source-local structural support, but “Aleph/Beth ... headings” is false for the scoped XML. Use initial-letter evidence and preserve the whole poem.
4. Psalm 145 is incomplete as an acrostic in the scoped MT/UXLC evidence. No absent nun line, title, accent, Selah, or versification feature can be promoted into automatic child-boundary authority.
5. Psalm 95 is the clearest demonstration that a meaningful form label cannot cure a syntactically severed sentence.
